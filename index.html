This entry is for someone under 18, so we haven't applied adult categories — they would be misleading while growth is still in progress. For ages 2–17, BMI is interpreted with age- and sex-specific percentile charts (BMI-for-age), best reviewed with a pediatric clinician.

How to read your result

A screening measure, by design

BMI divides weight by height squared (kg/m²). It was built to compare populations, not to diagnose individuals. A result outside the reference range is a prompt for a fuller conversation — not a verdict. Clinicians combine it with waist measurements, medical history, lab results, and examination before saying anything about your health.

Equally, a result inside the reference range is not a clean bill of health. Treat the number as one data point, and bring questions to a qualified professional.

What BMI can't tell you

  • Composition

    BMI can't separate fat from muscle or bone, so muscular people can land in a higher category with low body fat, and others can sit in the reference range with high body fat.

  • Distribution

    Where fat is carried matters for risk. Waist circumference adds context that a single BMI number cannot.

  • Life stage

    Standard cut-offs are less informative during pregnancy, for many adults over 65, and for children and teens (who need BMI-for-age percentiles).

  • Ancestry differences

    Health risk can begin at different BMI points across ethnic groups; some public-health bodies discuss lower action points for some populations. A clinician can interpret this for you.

  • Individual variation

    Fitness, medications, conditions, disability, and history all change what a number means. That interpretation needs a person, not a formula.

  • One moment in time

    A single reading is a snapshot. Trends — discussed with a professional — are more informative than any one result.

Disclaimer

BMIBrief is an educational screening aid for adults. It is not medical advice, not a diagnostic tool, and not a medical device. BMI is a screening measure, not a diagnosis. Never start, stop, or change any treatment, diet, or exercise program based on a number from this page — talk with a qualified health professional who knows your history.

Children and teens (2–17): adult BMI categories do not apply. Please use BMI-for-age percentile charts and consult a pediatric clinician. Helpful starting points: CDC child & teen BMI and the WHO child growth standards.

If weight or eating is a source of distress for you, consider reaching out to a health professional or an eating-disorder support service. Numbers on a page should never add pressure to your day.
